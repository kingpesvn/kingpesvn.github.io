Free booking website: 6 parts and templates by industry

How to make a free booking website for a spa, nail salon, gym, dental office or dive center: priced services, open slots, a form that goes to text.

“Do you have anything tomorrow?” arrives every day, and then you have to ask back: which service, how many people, what time. This guide shows how to build a free booking website so customers see your services, prices and open times and send a complete request in one go. It works for spas, nail salons, gyms, clinics, dive centers and most service businesses.

Quick summary, 6 parts you need:

  1. Services with price and duration
  2. Pick a day and an open time
  3. A booking form that goes to text, Google Forms or email
  4. Opening hours, reminders and a cancellation policy
  5. Booking templates by industry
  6. Your booking link everywhere customers find you

1. Services with price and duration

Customers want two things before they book: how much, and how long. List each service with its price, duration and one short line, like “Hot stone massage · 60 min · $95”. If you price by package or by session, say so.

It works like the computer reservation systems airlines and hotels use, only simpler: the customer picks a service first, then a time. Every request you receive already names the service, so you never have to ask.

Spas and massage studios should show the length of each treatment so people pick the right slot. Photo: Frank Denney (CC0).

2. Pick a day and an open time

Offer only the times you really take, such as 9:00, 10:30, 2:00 and 4:00. Hide days that are full or closed. For classes or boat trips with fixed times, show the weekly schedule with spots left.

Do not let people type a time. Choosing from a list saves you from “sometime in the afternoon” and cuts the back-and-forth.

3. A booking form that goes to text, Google Forms or email

The form needs a few fields: service, date, time, name, phone, notes. On submit, the request goes to a Google Form you read as a spreadsheet, or opens a pre-filled WhatsApp, text or email message.

Nail salons can let customers write the color or design in the notes field. Photo: Kris Atomic (CC0).

Add one question that matters for your trade: a nail salon asks about gel removal, a dentist asks about pain, a dive center asks for certification. One question is enough; more and people give up.

4. Opening hours, reminders and a cancellation policy

Show hours for each day and a live “Open now” or “Closed now”. Someone booking late at night still knows when you open tomorrow.

Write your cancellation and late policy in a line or two: how much notice, how many minutes late before you reschedule. When you confirm, resend the date, time and address, and send one reminder before the visit to cut no-shows.

Gyms and yoga studios usually book by class: show the weekly timetable, spots left and a free trial. Photo: Jan Laugesen (CC0).

6. Your booking link everywhere customers find you

A booking website only helps if people see the link. Put it on your Google Business Profile, Facebook page, Instagram bio, TikTok and email signature. When someone messages to ask about times, send the link instead of listing slots.

Frequently asked questions

Can a free booking website take deposits?

A static website does not charge cards by itself. The simple way is to show a payment link or bank details in the confirmation, and ask the customer to send a screenshot with the booking message.

Where do booking requests go?

To a Google Form (you read them in Google Sheets), or into a pre-filled WhatsApp, text or email message with the service, date, time, name and phone. You confirm with one reply.

How do I stop two people booking the same slot?

Only open the slots you really take, note how many spots each slot has and always confirm before the visit. Busy shops can mark full slots in a Google Sheet.

Do customers need an app to book?

No. They open your link on their phone and book, with no app and no account. Put the link on Google Maps, Instagram, Facebook and your email signature.
