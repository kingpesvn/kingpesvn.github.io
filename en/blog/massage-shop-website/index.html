Massage shop website: prices by minute and online booking

A massage shop website needs prices for 60/90/120 minutes, treatments by sore spot, pressure levels, booking by free slot and health notes. Build it free.

People looking for a massage usually open their phone after work, shoulders stiff and back aching, and want to know three things right away: how much, how long, and is there a slot tonight. The shop that answers clearly online gets the call first. A tidy massage shop website lets clients see prices by duration, pick the treatment that fits where it hurts and book in a minute. Here are the 7 sections it should have and how to build it for free.

Quick summary: a massage shop website should have:

  1. Prices for 60, 90 and 120 minutes
  2. Treatment suggestions by sore spot
  3. Pressure levels, explained up front
  4. What happens in a session
  5. A booking form based on free slots
  6. Health notes and “before you come”
  7. Packages, gift vouchers and reviews

1. Prices for 60, 90 and 120 minutes

Clients compare massage shops on two numbers: duration and price. List every treatment with 60, 90 and 120 minutes, each with its own price, for example Thai massage $45 for 60 minutes and $60 for 90. Add one line on what the price includes: ginger tea, hot towels, a change of clothes, a shower, and whether there is a service charge.

A price for each duration lets clients choose by the time they have. Photo: Healthy Living (CC0).

Group the treatments: Thai massage, oil and hot stone, neck, shoulder and back therapy, foot massage and hair wash, combos and couples. Each treatment gets a photo, one line of description and a book-now button.

2. Treatment suggestions by sore spot

Many clients can’t tell a Thai massage from a deep tissue one. They only know “my neck and shoulders hurt”, “my legs are tired”, “I can’t sleep”. Add a “Where does it hurt today?” section with tap-to-choose areas. Tap one and the matching treatments light up, with a tip such as “lots of desk work: try the 60-minute neck and shoulder therapy”.

It helps clients choose quickly and shows that the shop knows its craft. New clients often book exactly the treatment suggested.

3. Pressure levels, explained up front

First-timers worry that massage will hurt, regulars worry it will be too soft. Explain three pressure levels: light for relaxing and sleeping well, medium for most people, firm for tight muscles and those used to massage. Let clients choose the level when they book.

Clear pressure levels and a cup of tea before the session put first-time clients at ease. Photo: Rawpixel (CC0).

Add a promise: the therapist checks the pressure after the first 5 minutes, and clients can ask for lighter pressure at any time.

4. What happens in a session

Describe the 4 or 5 steps of a session: arrive 10 minutes early to fill in a short health note, a ginger foot soak, changing, the massage, then rest with hot tea. New clients who know what to expect feel less nervous and arrive on time.

If your shop follows traditional Thai massage, you can link to the Thai massage page on Wikipedia for visitors who want to learn more.

5. A booking form based on free slots

A good booking form shows only the start times that leave enough time before closing. For example, if the shop closes at 11 pm and the client picks 90 minutes, the latest start is 9:30 pm. Past times today are hidden; if today is full, the form picks tomorrow automatically.

The fields you need: treatment, duration, date, time, number of guests (1 or 2 for a couples room), female or male therapist, pressure, focus areas, name and phone number. The form works out the price from the duration and the number of guests. When sent, the booking goes into Google Sheets through Google Forms or becomes a pre-filled WhatsApp message. The shop calls to confirm within minutes.

6. Health notes and “before you come”

Add a few health tick boxes to the form: pregnant, high blood pressure, recent injury, allergic to essential oils. The therapist knows in advance and can adjust the technique and avoid areas. State clearly that massage is not a substitute for medical treatment.

A short “before you come” list helps the massage start on time and safely. Photo: Rawpixel (CC0).

A short “Before you come” section: don’t eat a big meal in the hour before, arriving more than 15 minutes late may shorten the session, lockers and showers are available, payment is after the massage. These lines cut down on phone questions a lot.

7. Packages, gift vouchers and reviews

Regulars are steady income. Sell 5- and 10-session packages cheaper than single visits, and massage gift vouchers for birthdays, Mother’s Day and Valentine’s. Each package gets a button to message the shop and buy.

Copy a few real Google reviews onto the website, especially ones that name the treatment and how it felt. Set up your profile with the guide to add your business to Google Maps so anyone searching “massage near me” sees your website link. If your shop also does nails, read the nail salon website guide.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small massage shop need a website?

Yes, if you want clients to book ahead. People looking for a massage usually check prices, durations and free times on their phone before they call. One page with a clear price list and a booking form helps them choose you faster, especially in the evening and at weekends.

Should I price massage per session or per minute?

By duration, for example 60, 90 and 120 minutes, each with its own price. Clients compare easily and pick by how much time they have. List combos and multi-session packages separately.

Should clients be able to choose a male or female therapist?

Yes. Many clients, especially women, only book when they are sure of a female therapist. A "female, male or no preference" choice on the form puts them at ease.

Can a website replace the shop's appointment book?

Not completely. The form only sends a request; the shop still calls to confirm and writes it in its own calendar. If you take bookings through Google Forms, every appointment lands in one spreadsheet, ready for assigning therapists.
