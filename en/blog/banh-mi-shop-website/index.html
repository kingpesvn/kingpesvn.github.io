Banh mi shop website: take bulk orders from offices

A banh mi shop website wins office orders: a priced menu, bake times, bulk discounts, delivery slots and company invoices. Build one free in 5 minutes.

In the morning, a banh mi shop is busy with commuters grabbing one sandwich each. The big orders, though, come from offices: a tray for a meeting, lunch for a training day, a small birthday party. The person ordering usually searches on Google and calls whichever shop looks clearest. A tidy banh mi shop website lets them see the menu and prices, work out quantities and send an order in a few minutes. Here are the 7 sections it should have and how to build it for free.

Quick summary: a banh mi shop website should have:

  1. A menu with prices and a photo of each filling
  2. The bake time of every batch
  3. A bulk discount table
  4. An office order form that adds up the total
  5. Delivery slots, delivery area and notice needed
  6. Names on each sandwich and company invoices
  7. Opening hours, map and reviews

1. A menu with prices and a photo of each filling

Someone ordering for an office is choosing for ten people or more. They need to know which fillings you have, what each costs, and whether there are vegetarian or mild options. Group the menu: filled banh mi, plain bread and pâté, drinks, combos.

A photo of each filling helps the person ordering choose quickly for the whole team. Photo: Phương Huy (CC0).

Give each item one line of ingredients and one clear price. If an item comes in two sizes, show both prices. Add small tags such as “Spicy”, “Vegetarian” and “New” so people can scan the list. End the menu with one line: prices include herbs, pickles and sauce; add a note for an extra egg or no onion.

2. The bake time of every batch

Banh mi is best straight out of the oven, and customers know it. List the batch times for the day, for example 5:30, 9:30, 15:00 and 17:30. The website can highlight the next batch based on the real time and show “in 40 minutes”. Passers-by know when to drop in for the warmest bread.

For office orders, bake times are also a promise: a 10:00 delivery comes from the 9:30 batch. Say so in the ordering section so the organiser knows the bread will still be crisp when it reaches their colleagues.

3. A bulk discount table

People ordering for a company always ask “is there a discount for a big order?” Instead of answering every message, publish a bulk discount table: 5% off from 20 sandwiches, 10% off from 50, 15% off from 100. These numbers are only an example; set your own based on your margin.

A bulk discount table answers “is there a discount?” before anyone asks. Photo: cocoparisienne (CC0).

A clear table also has another effect: a customer ordering 45 sees that 5 more gets them 10% off, and often adds them. It is even easier if the website reminds them: “add 5 more to get 10% off”.

4. An office order form that adds up the total

An office order has many items, each with its own quantity. Typing it all into a WhatsApp message is easy to get wrong. A good form lists the items with minus / plus buttons and shows the subtotal, discount, delivery fee and total as they go.

The details section only needs the delivery date and time, recipient, phone, company, address (building, floor, room) and notes. When they press send, the order becomes a row in Google Sheets through Google Forms, or a ready-made WhatsApp message. The shop calls to confirm before baking.

5. Delivery slots, delivery area and notice needed

The next three questions are: what time can you deliver, do you deliver to me, and how far ahead must I order? Give specific delivery slots, for example every 30 minutes from 6:30 to 11:30 and from 14:00 to 16:00 in the afternoon. Describe the delivery area by district or by radius.

Stating the notice you need gives the shop time to make the dough and bake. Photo: Beboplu (CC0).

Set the notice by order size: under 50 sandwiches, 2 hours ahead; 50 or more, by 17:00 the day before. The form can block slots that are too close, so nobody orders at 9:00 for a 9:30 delivery.

6. Names on each sandwich and company invoices

Two small details bring offices back: writing a name on each wrapper so nothing gets mixed up, and issuing a company invoice. On the website, add an “invoice needed” tick box and a hint in the notes field: “send the list of names and items”.

Also explain how to pay: cash or bank transfer on delivery, and company orders paid by transfer after the invoice arrives. The organiser is often an office administrator who needs to tell accounts in advance.

7. Opening hours, map and reviews

Walk-in customers are still most of your takings each morning. List your opening hours, address and a map, and set up your profile with the guide to add your business to Google Maps. When someone searches “banh mi near me”, a profile with your website link lets them check the menu before they come.

Copy a few real Google reviews onto the website, especially from office customers: “delivered 15 minutes early and the bread was still crisp”. If you also sell cakes and pastries, read the bakery website checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small banh mi shop need a website?

Yes, if you want office orders. People ordering for a meeting usually search on Google and want to see the menu, prices and how to order before they call. One page with all of that helps them choose you faster.

How much discount should I give on large orders?

Many shops use 2 or 3 tiers, for example 5% off from 20 sandwiches and 10% off from 50. The numbers depend on your margin; what matters is showing them on the website so customers can work it out and top up to the next tier.

How far ahead should offices order?

It depends on your oven capacity. For example, orders under 50 need 2 hours' notice and bigger orders need to be in by the afternoon before. Show the cut-off time on the form so nobody orders too close to delivery.

Do I need online payment?

Not necessarily. Most banh mi shops take cash or a bank transfer on delivery. Companies often need an invoice before they pay, so a "company invoice needed" box on the form is more useful than a payment gateway.
