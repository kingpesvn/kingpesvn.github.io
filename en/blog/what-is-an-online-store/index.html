What is an online store? A simple guide for beginners

What is an online store, what parts does it have, how is it different from social pages and marketplaces, and what you need to start. Explained simply.

You sell on Facebook, Instagram or a marketplace, and people keep telling you to “get a website”. So what is an online store exactly? Put simply, it’s a website that belongs to your shop, where customers browse products, see prices and order without messaging you about every item. This guide explains the parts of an online store, how it differs from social pages and marketplaces, and what a beginner needs.

Quick summary, 5 things to know about an online store:

  1. It has a home page, categories, product pages, a cart or order form, and contact details
  2. Unlike social pages and marketplaces, you own it
  3. Customers buy in 5 steps: find, browse, choose, order, receive
  4. You need a domain, hosting, content and a way to take orders
  5. Selling online may come with rules where you are

1. What parts does an online store have?

An online store is a form of e-commerce owned by the shop itself. A basic one usually has:

  • Home page: who you are, what you sell, a clear order button.
  • Categories and filters: by product type, price, size.
  • Product pages: photos, description, price, colour or size options.
  • Cart or order form: customers pick items and enter name, phone and address.
  • Contact and policies: opening hours, map, returns, delivery.

The cart on a website does the same job as a shopping basket. Photo: leonie wise (CC0).

2. How is it different from social pages and marketplaces?

On a social page, posts scroll away and customers have to message you for prices. On a marketplace, you sell alongside thousands of other shops under its rules and fees. An online store is your “own shop”: you choose the look, prices and ordering, and visitors only see your products.

You don’t have to pick just one. Many shops use social media to find customers, marketplaces for extra orders, and their website as the place customers come back to order directly. See the full comparison in website vs Facebook page.

3. How does a purchase work?

A purchase usually takes 5 steps: the customer finds you (via Google, social media or a QR code), browses products, picks a size or colour, places the order and receives it. The fewer words and buttons at each step, the fewer customers drop off.

Ordering on a website is like the checkout counter: the simpler the better. Photo: Sprinno (CC0).

Small shops don’t need online payment straight away. Customers order through a cart that sends a message or a Google Form; you call to confirm and take payment by transfer or cash on delivery.

4. What do you need to start one?

Four basics: a domain (your web address), hosting so the site runs 24/7, content (photos, descriptions, prices) and a way to take orders. You can start with a free address; see free domain for your shop. Costs are broken down in how much an online store website costs.

Once the order is in, what’s left is packing and delivery. Photo: DPLA (Public Domain).

For beginners, the hardest part is usually photos and descriptions, not the tech. Shoot with your phone near a window and write short descriptions with material, size and price, and you’re ready to start.

5. Do you need to register an online store?

Rules depend on the country. In Vietnam, for example, an online store may have to be notified to the Ministry of Industry and Trade depending on the case. Rules change, so check official sources or ask the authority where you sell. The Vietnam cases are summarised in do you need to register an e-commerce website in Vietnam.

How should a beginner start?

The quickest route is to pick a ready-made template for your trade, swap in your text, photos and prices, and publish. For example, the souvenir shop template has a cart and gift wrap; if you sell services, the ski school template or the physiotherapy clinic template let customers pick a lesson or a time and book straight away.

Frequently asked questions

Does an online store need online payment?

No. Many small shops take orders through a cart that sends a message or a Google Form, then accept bank transfer or cash on delivery. Add online payment later if needed.

What’s the difference between an online store and a showcase website?

A showcase site mainly shows information. An online store adds prices and a cart or order form so customers can order right on the page.

Can I build an online store without coding?

Yes. With a ready-made template you only change text, photos, prices and how you take orders; the cart and mobile layout are already done.

Is a site that sells services (bookings, classes) an online store?

You can think of it that way: customers choose a service, see the price and book on the page. The cart is simply replaced by a date and time picker.
