Women's Day Promotion Ideas for Spas, Bakeries and Cafés

Easy Women's Day and Mother's Day promotion ideas for spas, bakeries and cafés, with a prep timeline, gift vouchers and how to promote offers online.

International Women’s Day (8 March) and Mother’s Day are when many people look for gifts for their mum, partner, friends or colleagues: flowers, a cake, a spa treatment or a coffee date. This article collects easy Women’s Day promotion ideas for small spas, bakeries and cafés, with a prep timeline and ways to reach customers without paying for ads. The same ideas work for Mother’s Day.

In short, a Women’s Day promotion plan has 7 parts:

  1. Plan 2–3 weeks ahead
  2. Ideas for spas
  3. Ideas for bakeries
  4. Ideas for cafés
  5. Sell gift vouchers
  6. Put the offer on your website and social media
  7. Mistakes to avoid

1. Plan 2–3 weeks ahead

Holiday promotions often fail because they’re rushed: not enough stock, no free slots, customers find out too late. A simple timeline:

  • About 3 weeks before: pick 1–2 main offers, calculate costs, order ingredients, gift boxes and cards.
  • About 2 weeks before: take real photos, write the copy, create an offer page on your website.
  • 7–10 days before: announce the offer, open pre-orders, message your regulars.
  • 2–3 days before: send a final reminder with a clear order deadline.
  • On the day: have enough staff and stock; mark items “sold out” as soon as they are.

A few clear offers are easier to run and sell better than ten small ones.

2. Ideas for spas

Spas have a big advantage: self-care is a gift many people want to give.

A treatment room prepared with care, a flower on the towel: small details make a gift package feel special. Photo: bloggeratlarge (CC0).

A few ideas:

  • “For Mum” or “For Her” package: a facial or massage with tea and a small gift.
  • Duo package: mother and daughter, sisters, best friends, priced a little below two single bookings.
  • Add-ons instead of discounts: book the main treatment and get an extra 15-minute neck and shoulder massage or a mask.
  • Early-booking perk: encourage people to book ahead so you can plan shifts and avoid a rush on the day itself.

3. Ideas for bakeries

Cakes and pastry boxes are classic gifts. Suggestions:

  • A special cake design: buttercream flowers in pink, red or yellow; state the size and how many it serves.
  • Cake + flowers + card set: customers don’t need to visit a florist, and you increase the order value.
  • Small pastry boxes for colleagues: 4–6 pastries in a nice box, great for office orders.
  • Free writing on pre-orders and a chosen pickup time, so you know quantities in advance.

Always state the order deadline (for example two days before) and cap daily orders to what your kitchen can handle.

A small cake with pink icing and a rose petal: simple, and just right for the occasion. Photo: Andy Li (CC0).

4. Ideas for cafés

A café doesn’t sell expensive gifts, but it can be where people bring someone special on the day:

  • A seasonal drink sold only that week, with a memorable name.
  • A small extra: a flower, a card or a cookie with every drink.
  • Buy 2, get 1 free or a group discount for 3 or more, ideal for friends and colleagues.
  • A small photo corner with flowers and a greeting sign: customers post photos and mention your café for free.

A heart on a cup of coffee costs nothing extra, but customers remember it. Photo: Londonjackbooks (CC0).

5. Sell gift vouchers

Vouchers are a handy gift for people who live far away or don’t know what to choose. Spas, bakeries and cafés can all offer them.

Vouchers or pre-wrapped gift boxes make buying easy for the giver and bring new customers after the holiday. Photo: Porapak Apichodilok (CC0).

How to set them up:

  • By value (for example $25) or by service (one treatment, one 6-inch cake).
  • A unique code and a clear expiry date (for example 3 months) with short terms.
  • Let people buy online and receive it by message: they pay, you send an image of the voucher with its code.
  • Track sold and redeemed vouchers in a simple spreadsheet to avoid mistakes.

Vouchers also bring revenue up front and new customers after the holiday.

6. Put the offer on your website and social media

A great offer is useless if nobody knows about it. Free channels:

  • Website: add a “Women’s Day offer” section or page with real photos, prices, the deadline and message / call buttons to order. This is the link you share everywhere else.
  • Instagram, Facebook, TikTok: post real product photos, pin the offer post, put your website link in your bio.
  • Messaging apps: send regulars one short message with the link, not a flood.
  • Google Maps: add photos and an offer post on your Google Business Profile (free).
  • At the counter: a small card or sign with a QR code to the offer page.

7. Mistakes to avoid

  • Discounting too deeply and losing money, or training regulars to wait for sales.
  • No deadline or quantity limit, then taking more orders than you can handle.
  • Using stock photos for the products on offer: customers are disappointed when the real thing looks different.
  • Forgetting to remove the offer afterwards, so people order at old prices.
  • Not keeping a list of buyers, missing the chance to invite them back for the next holiday.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start a Women's Day or Mother's Day promotion?

Announce your offer about 7–10 days before the day and take pre-orders from then. Many people buy gifts early, especially cakes and gift vouchers.

How big should the discount be?

There's no universal number. Instead of a deep discount, add something extra (a card, a flower, a small gift) or create a bundle, so customers feel the value and you don't lose money. Work out your costs first to know your maximum discount.

How can a small shop with no ad budget promote it?

Put the offer on your website and pin it on your social pages, message your regulars, place a small card at the counter and post the offer on your Google Business Profile.

What should I do with the offer page after the day?

Remove it or mark it "ended" right after the last day so nobody orders by mistake. Keep a list of buyers so you can invite them back for the next occasion.
