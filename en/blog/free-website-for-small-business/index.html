Free website for small business: 6 must-have sections

Build a free website for small business: the 6 sections customers look for, from services, prices and service area to instant quotes and booking.

Many small business owners think a website is for big companies: expensive, needs a developer, hard to update. In reality, customers search Google before they call. This guide shows how to build a free website for small business with the 6 sections customers need to see, using two new templates as examples: a garden design studio and a wedding photographer.

Quick summary, 6 must-have sections:

  1. One line that says what you do and where
  2. Services and starting prices
  3. Photos of real work
  4. An instant quote or availability check
  5. Service area, hours and contact
  6. Reviews, FAQ and a booking button

1. One line that says what you do and where

Visitors give a page a few seconds. Your first line should answer: what you do, in which city, and what makes you different. The garden design template opens with “Garden design and build in Portland” plus 3 numbers: gardens completed, plant warranty and review score.

One great photo of your work at the top says more than a long introduction. Photo: Matt Bango (CC0).

Most companies in the US count as a small business, so customers always have options. A clear opening line keeps them on your page.

2. Services and starting prices

Showing prices, even just “from $…”, filters for the right budget and cuts down “how much?” messages. The garden template prices by area and switches square feet to square meters by country; the wedding photographer template lists packages by hours, with a “most booked” label. In the US, say prices are before sales tax.

Group your services: design, build, maintenance; or coverage packages and add-ons. Give each service a one-line description of what the customer gets.

3. Photos of real work

Real photos earn more trust than stock images. The garden template has before-and-after photos with a slider to compare; the wedding template has a full album for each wedding that opens full screen.

Product photos with prices, like this bakery counter, tell customers right away what they get for their money. Photo: Andy Li (CC0).

Your best 6 to 9 photos are enough. A phone camera works as long as the shot is bright and straight; see the tips in product photography with your phone.

4. An instant quote or availability check

This is what makes a small business website more than a flyer. The garden template has a budget calculator: enter the yard size, pick a finish level, the patio and lawn split, tick irrigation, lighting and a water feature, and see a price range instantly. The wedding photographer template lets couples pick their date to see if it’s open, then build a package with the total and the retainer.

For date-based services like weddings, the first question is always “are you free that day?”. Photo: Matt Bango (CC0).

The result is attached to the inquiry form, so you get a message that already includes the yard size, budget or wedding date. Read the guide to a wedding photographer website for details.

5. Service area, hours and contact

A service business should say exactly where it works: a list of neighborhoods or towns, or a radius in miles. Add your hours, a map, phone number, email and a message button. The template shows “open now” or “closed” in your time zone.

Updating hours, prices and open dates in the editor takes a few minutes a week. Photo: Photo Mix (CC0).

Don’t forget to put your business on the map: see how to add your business to Google Maps and link your website in the profile.

6. Reviews, FAQ and a booking button

Three to five real reviews, with a name and the service used, are more convincing than a bare star rating. An FAQ answers common questions before they’re asked: what’s included, how the deposit works, how long it takes.

Put the booking or quote button in the top bar and in a bottom bar on phones. If you need a fuller company site, see these free company website templates.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small business still need a website if it has a Facebook page?

Yes. A social page is good for news, but a website is what people find on Google, where they see prices, your service area and book without messaging you. Use both; the website is your permanent address.

What are the limits of a free website?

With a Kingpes template, your site is hosted free on GitHub Pages, with no ads and no expiry. The main limit is that you update it yourself; a custom domain costs a yearly fee.

Can I build it if I can't code?

Yes. Pick a template for your trade, change the text, prices and photos in the editor, preview on desktop and phone, then publish. Nothing to install.

How does a website help a service business that doesn't sell online?

Service customers want to know what you do, roughly what it costs, whether you cover their area and when you're free. A website answers all four and sends requests to your email, WhatsApp or Google Forms.
