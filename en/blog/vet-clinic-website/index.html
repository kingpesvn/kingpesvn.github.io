Vet clinic website: 7 sections that win pet owners' trust

A vet clinic website needs an emergency number, prices by dog, cat and weight, a vaccine schedule from birth date and online booking. Build it free.

Pet owners usually look for a clinic when they are most worried: the dog stopped eating, the cat is vomiting, or a vaccine is due and nobody remembers which one. A clear vet clinic website shows the emergency number, guide prices and opening hours at a glance and lets owners book in a few minutes, even when the front desk is busy. Here are the 7 sections it should have and how to build it for free.

Quick summary: a vet clinic website needs 7 sections:

  1. The emergency number always at the top
  2. Prices by dog, cat and weight
  3. A vaccine schedule from the pet’s birth date
  4. Online booking that asks about symptoms
  5. Your vets and how a visit works
  6. Vaccine and check-up reminders by message
  7. Your clinic on Google Maps

1. The emergency number always at the top

When a pet is in trouble, owners have no time to scroll for a phone number. Put an emergency strip at the very top with your on-call hours, for example “Dog and cat emergencies 24/7”, and a number that can be tapped to call on a phone.

On mobile, add an emergency call button to the bar at the bottom of the screen, next to the booking button. If you are not open at night, don’t write 24/7. State the hours you take emergencies and suggest the nearest place owners can go after hours.

2. Prices by dog, cat and weight

The question owners ask most is “how much?”. Many services, such as spay or neuter, dental scaling or boarding, cost different amounts by species and weight. Split your price list into groups: exams, vaccines, spay and neuter, lab tests and imaging, prevention and care.

Let owners tap dog or cat and their pet’s weight band, so the list shows only the column they need. Say clearly that these are guide prices, whether the exam fee is included, and add “the vet gives you a quote before any tests or extra treatment”. One line like that reassures owners far more than leaving it blank.

3. A vaccine schedule from the pet’s birth date

Owners of puppies and kittens rarely remember when the next shot is due. A vaccine schedule box on your website solves that: the owner enters the pet’s birth date, the page works out the expected date of each shot and highlights the next one.

A schedule based on birth date shows owners the next shot and lets them book it right away. Photo: Rawpixel (CC0).

The clinic enters the schedule itself: vaccine name, age and age in weeks. Add the line “Guide only, the vet examines your pet and adjusts the schedule to its health” right below. Next to each shot, put a “Book this shot” button that fills in the species, vaccine and date in the booking form.

4. Online booking that asks about symptoms

A short booking form means fewer calls for the front desk and lets the vet know what is going on before the pet arrives. The form should ask for:

  • Pet details: dog or cat, weight, name, age.
  • Service: exam, vaccine, spay or neuter, or “not sure, I need advice”.
  • Preferred date and time and a symptoms box, for example “not eating for 2 days, vomiting”.
  • A home visit checkbox if you offer it, which reveals an address field when ticked.

A booking form with symptoms helps the vet prepare before the pet arrives. Photo: Negative Space (CC0).

When the owner taps send, the request reaches the clinic’s WhatsApp or becomes a row in Google Sheets. See the Google Forms help guide for how to build the form. To track appointments in a spreadsheet, read managing court bookings with Google Sheets; the same method works for vet appointments.

5. Your vets and how a visit works

Owners are handing their pet to strangers, so they want to know who will treat it. Give each vet a name, role, focus such as internal medicine, surgery or ultrasound, and years of experience. If you don’t have good photos yet, initials in a coloured circle look tidy enough.

Add a how a visit works section in 4 steps: book or walk in, history and physical exam, quote before treatment, treatment and follow-up date. Owners then know what to prepare and won’t be surprised by the bill.

6. Vaccine and check-up reminders by message

Many owners forget the yearly booster or the follow-up date. When bookings come in through WhatsApp or text, the clinic already has the number to send a reminder a day or two before. Say so on your website in the promises section, for example “Vaccine and check-up reminders by WhatsApp”.

Regular reminders keep pets fully vaccinated and bring owners back to the clinic. Photo: Freddie Marriage (CC0).

The promises section is also the place for what owners care about: separate waiting areas for dogs and cats, sterilised instruments for each patient, an observation ward and the payment methods you accept.

7. Your clinic on Google Maps

Lots of people search “vet near me” on their phone. Set up your Google Maps profile with the guide to adding your business to Google Maps, and add your website link there and on the clinic’s Facebook page.

Put a QR code for your shop that links to your website at the front desk and in the pet’s vaccine booklet: next time the owner just scans it to book the next shot.

Frequently asked questions

Should a vet clinic publish prices on its website?

Yes, as a guide for common services such as exams, vaccines and spay or neuter, with the line "the vet gives you a quote before any tests or extra treatment". Owners feel safer and call less to ask prices.

Can a vaccine schedule on the website replace the vet's advice?

No. It only tells owners roughly when to bring their pet in. Say clearly that it is a guide and the vet adjusts it to each pet's health and vaccine history.

Can online bookings clash?

A simple website does not lock time slots. Each request reaches your WhatsApp or Google Sheets, the front desk checks and confirms. State how fast you confirm, for example within 15 minutes during opening hours.

Does a small clinic with one vet need a website?

It helps if it's free. One page with the emergency number, prices, hours and a booking button helps new owners find you on Google and cuts repeat questions on the phone.
