Costume rental website: free template, 6 must-have parts

How to build a costume rental website for free: a catalog filtered by size and free dates, a size finder, pickup and return dates, deposit, reservations.

Costume renters ask three questions: is this one free on my date, what size do I wear, and what is the total including the deposit. Answering by message takes the whole afternoon, and customers try another shop while they wait. This guide shows how to build a costume rental website for free, for cosplay, theater and party costumes, so customers can see what is free, find their size and send a complete reservation.

Quick summary, 6 must-have parts:

  1. A catalog with real photos, filtered by type and size
  2. Availability by event date
  3. A size finder from height and chest
  4. A rental calculator: pickup, return, deposit
  5. Rules for cleaning, late returns and deposits
  6. A reservation form with everything you need

1. A catalog with real photos, filtered by type and size

One card per costume: a real photo on a mannequin or rack, the name, available sizes, what is included (helmet, gloves, props) and the 3-day and 7-day price. Customers filter by type, like armor, historical gowns, 1920s or fantasy, then by size.

The cosplay community often mixes rented pieces with homemade accessories, so noting which pieces can be split up is a plus.

2. Availability by event date

This is what customers need most. They enter their event date and each card says “free” or “booked, next free date is…”. The new template adds your cleaning days between rentals, so you never rent out a costume that has not been cleaned.

A small tile at the top does the same at a glance: pick a date and a type, and the page counts how many costumes are free. Someone heading to a con this weekend knows right away whether to book with you.

3. A size finder from height and chest

Customers enter height and chest and get a size. If the two land on different sizes, the page suggests the larger one, because straps and lacing can be taken in at pickup.

Historical gowns do not stretch: a chest-based size chart helps customers choose right the first time. Photo: The Met (CC0).

The chart is stored in cm; the US market switches to inches automatically. “Show costumes in my size” filters the catalog right away.

4. A rental calculator: pickup, return, deposit

Customers pick a costume, the event date, 3 or 7 days, in-store pickup or shipping, and extras like wig styling, makeup or temporary alterations. The page shows:

  • Pickup date: the day before the event, or the day you ship.
  • Return by: pickup date plus rental days.
  • Free or booked, with the next free date.
  • Rental, shipping, refundable deposit and the total due today.

Armor usually carries a higher deposit; show it per piece so nobody is surprised. Photo: The Met (CC0).

“Reserve this costume” copies the whole selection into the form; the customer only adds a name and phone.

5. Rules for cleaning, late returns and deposits

Keep it short, one rule per line: pick up before the event, return by the due date, the late fee per day, no fake blood or glitter glue on fabric, props are light and convention-safe. Say clearly “cleaning is included, no need to wash”.

Add how many days the deposit takes to come back and what is free, like a loose seam or a lost button. Customers who read this first have nothing to argue about at return.

6. A reservation form with everything you need

The form asks for the costume, event date, size, shipping address (blank for in-store pickup), name, phone and notes. Without a date or phone number it asks again.

Notes like height or what to adjust let the shop prepare before the customer arrives. Photo: Igor Ovsyannykov (CC0).

Reservations go to Google Forms, email or a ready-made message. See it live in the costume rental template; it is set in the US with a Japanese version.

Temporary alterations are a small extra that keeps customers coming back. Photo: Annie Spratt (CC0).

Frequently asked questions

Does a small costume rental shop need a website if it has social media?

Yes. Social media is good for showing new pieces; a website answers what customers ask most: is it free on my date, which size fits, how many days, how much is the deposit.

How do I keep availability accurate?

Each costume has a list of booked dates. When someone reserves, add the dates in the editor. The site adds your cleaning days automatically and shows the next free date.

Should I show the deposit?

Yes. Showing the deposit for each costume, how fast it comes back and what can be deducted builds trust and avoids arguments at return.

Can I rent licensed character costumes?

Be careful. Costumes of film or comic characters can involve copyright and trademarks. Many shops rent original, historical and generic pieces that customers combine with their own accessories.
