Do free websites have ads? What to know first

Do free websites have ads? The kinds of ads and platform branding you will meet, which builders add them and which don’t, plus 6 checks before you choose.

By Kingpes · Published November 8, 2026 · 3 min read

When looking for a way to build a site for nothing, many shop owners ask: do free websites have ads? It depends. Some platforms put an ad bar on every page, some only add a small line at the bottom, some add nothing at all. This guide helps you know before your site goes live.

Quick summary, 5 things to know:

  1. Why free websites often carry ads
  2. The kinds of “ads” you will meet
  3. Who adds them and who doesn’t
  4. 6 checks before you choose
  5. What an ad-free website looks like

1. Why do free websites often carry ads?

Running websites costs servers, bandwidth and engineers. A platform that lets you build for free has to earn money somewhere, and online advertising is the easiest way: put ads on your pages, or nudge you to upgrade to a paid plan.

Customers open your shop site on a phone, and anything squeezed onto a small screen distracts them. Photo: Kristin Hardwick (CC0).

That is not bad in itself; you just need to know how you are “paying”.

2. The kinds of “ads” you will meet

It is not always a big banner. Watch for the lighter kinds too:

  • The platform’s ad bar at the top or bottom of every page, inviting visitors to build a site like yours.
  • Third-party ads between your content, sometimes from competitors.
  • A subdomain with the platform’s name, like yourshop.platform.com.
  • A small “Made with…” line at the bottom.
  • Upgrade prompts shown to you in the admin; customers don’t see them.

The first two affect customers most; the last three are usually fine for a new shop.

3. Who adds them and who doesn’t?

Based on each platform’s pricing at the time of writing (it can change, so check again before you build):

  • Wix free plan: a Wix ad bar on every page and an address like yourname.wixsite.com.
  • WordPress.com free plan: usually shows platform ads; see free WordPress website.
  • Google Sites: no ads, but few selling tools.
  • Kingpes: no ads on your website; a small “Made with Kingpes” line at the bottom that you can switch off in the editor.

Before choosing, open a real site built on that platform to see what customers will see. Photo: Alejandro Escamilla (CC0).

A fuller comparison of domains, orders and limits is in Kingpes vs Google Sites vs Wix.

4. What to check before you choose

Before you spend time entering products, check these 6 things:

  1. Open a sample site on your phone: where are the ads, do they cover the order button?
  2. What does the address look like, and can you connect your own domain?
  3. How much a year does removing ads cost?
  4. Can you take your text and photos with you if you leave?
  5. Limits on storage, pages and products.
  6. How customers order: chat apps, phone, a form or a cart.

5. What does an ad-free website look like?

Visitors only see your products and services. For example, the camera store template has just products, a kit builder and a bag; the tax accountant template has just fees, a deadline calendar and a contact form.

No one else’s ads, so customers aren’t pulled away to a competitor.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Kingpes website show ads?

No. Kingpes does not put ads on your website. There is only a small "Made with Kingpes" line at the bottom, which you can turn off in the editor.

Why do some free builders add ads?

Servers cost money. Ads or upgrade prompts are the usual way platforms pay for the free part.

How do I remove ads from a free website?

Usually by moving to the platform’s paid plan. Check the pricing before you start, because switching platforms later takes more work.

Does a "made with" line at the bottom matter?

Hardly for orders. It only shows which tool built the site, and many platforms let you hide it.
