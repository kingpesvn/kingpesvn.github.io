Print shop website: quantity price tiers and fast quotes

A print shop website needs a filtered catalogue, quantity price tiers, an instant estimate, delivery dates, a business quote form and clear file specs.

Print customers usually ask exactly three questions: how much for this many, when will it be ready, and how do I send the file. If staff have to work out every quote by hand, business clients will ask three other shops at the same time. A print shop website puts quantity price tiers and an instant estimate on the page, so customers check prices themselves and send a request with their file. Here are 7 things to include and how to build it for free.

In short, a print shop website should have:

  1. A filtered product catalogue
  2. Quantity price tiers
  3. An instant estimate by configuration
  4. Expected delivery dates and rush printing
  5. A quote form for businesses
  6. File specifications
  7. Capabilities, paper samples and reviews

1. A filtered product catalogue

Print shops make dozens of products: business cards, flyers, brochures, catalogues, paper bags, boxes, labels, calendars, banners. A left sidebar that filters by product group, print method (offset, digital, large format), finishing, turnaround and minimum order helps customers find exactly what they need.

Each product card shows 2–3 key specs (size, paper), working days and the unit price at large quantities. Customers can compare products without opening every page.

2. Quantity price tiers

This is what customers look for most. One table per product: 100, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000 pieces, with the total, the unit price per piece and the percentage saved against the smallest tier.

When they see 1,000 pieces cost only a little more than 500, customers often choose the bigger run. Add a note under the table: prices exclude tax, and the final quote follows the file check.

3. An instant estimate by configuration

Right below the price table, let customers choose size, paper, one or two sides, finishing (lamination, foil, die cut, embossing) and quantity. The estimate and unit price change the moment they click.

Show the extra cost of every option, for example “fine art paper +50%” or “foil + 0.01 per piece + die fee”. Customers weigh nicer against cheaper on their own and the shop explains less.

4. Expected delivery dates and rush printing

Next to the price, show the expected delivery date in working days, skipping Sundays. Digital printing can be same-day, offset 3–7 days, rigid boxes 10–12 days.

Add a rush option with its surcharge and the days saved. Businesses that need items for an event will pay more if they are sure of the date.

5. A quote form for businesses

The quote form only needs the fields the shop actually uses: company name, tax ID, contact person, phone, email for the quote, file link and notes. The configuration the customer just picked fills in the message automatically.

Sending via Google Form, email or a prefilled chat message all work. Say how quickly the shop replies with the official quote, for example within 30 minutes during office hours.

6. File specifications

A short checklist helps customers send the right file the first time: PDF or AI, text converted to outlines, CMYK colour, images at 300 dpi or more, 3 mm bleed, and text at least 3 mm from the trim line. Add a diagram of the bleed, trim line and safe area.

Mention that the shop checks files for free before printing. For background on offset printing, see Offset printing on Wikipedia.

7. Capabilities, paper samples and reviews

Show your machines with real photos and short specs: the sheet size of your 4-colour offset press, sheets per hour, the digital press and the large-format printer. List common papers (coated, ivory board, kraft, fine art) with weights and uses.

A few real reviews from business clients help newcomers trust you. Follow our guide to add your business to Google Maps so anyone searching “print shop near me” also finds your website link.

Frequently asked questions

Does a small print shop need a website?

Yes. Businesses search for "same-day business cards" or "cheap printed boxes" and pick whoever shows a price fastest. A website with quantity price tiers lets you collect quote requests even while the presses are running.

Should I publish print prices online?

Publish tiered prices for popular products such as business cards, flyers and paper bags. Make it clear these are estimates excluding tax and that the final quote follows the file check.

How should customers send print files?

The simplest way is to let customers paste a Google Drive or Dropbox link into the quote form. Print files are heavy, and a link is faster than an email attachment.

Why is the unit price lower for bigger runs?

Plate making, colour matching and press setup cost about the same for every job. A bigger run spreads that cost over more pieces. Explain this on the website so customers understand why ordering more pays off.
