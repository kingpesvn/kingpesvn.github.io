Chiropractor website: free template, 6 must-have parts

How to build a chiropractor website for free: where it hurts, treatments and visit counts, red flags, prices and insurance, and booking by open time.

People with back pain often search on their phone at midnight, when the pain keeps them awake. They want to know right away: do you treat this, how many visits, how much, and should they see a doctor first. This guide shows how to build a chiropractor website for free, for chiropractic, physiotherapy and massage therapy clinics, so patients find answers and book an open time themselves.

Quick summary, 6 parts you need:

  1. “Where does it hurt?”: problems by area and symptom
  2. Treatments and the usual number of visits
  3. Red flags: see a doctor first
  4. The first visit and home exercises
  5. Prices, care plans and insurance
  6. Booking by open time

1. “Where does it hurt?”: problems by area and symptom

Patients do not know the name of their condition, only where it hurts. Group common problems by area: neck, shoulder, upper back, low back, hip and leg, head, knee. Each problem gets a short card: how it feels, the usual number of visits and the matching service.

Add a row of symptom buttons like “stiff after a desk day” or “pain down one leg”. Tapping one lights up the matching problems. The Kingpes template also draws a spine on each card with the sore area highlighted, so you need no photo per problem.

Neck and shoulder stiffness after work is one of the most common reasons people look for a clinic. Photo: Jeff Sheldon (CC0).

2. Treatments and the usual number of visits

Chiropractic is more than adjustments. List each treatment you offer: adjustments, soft-tissue work, spinal decompression, sports injury rehab, prenatal care. For each, give the length, price and usual number of visits, such as “usually 3–6 visits”.

Clear visit counts help patients plan and trust you. Do not promise a cure; say you reassess after a few visits and refer on if there is no progress.

3. Red flags: see a doctor first

A short list of warning signs shows you put safety first: groin numbness or bladder changes, sudden limb weakness, back pain with fever, a recent fall or crash, the worst headache of your life.

Put the list in the treatments section and repeat it as a question in the booking form. If a patient ticks one, the page advises seeing a doctor or the ER first.

4. The first visit and home exercises

First-timers worry about what will happen. Write the first visit as 4–5 steps: forms before you come, talk and tests, a plan in plain words, gentle treatment if it is safe, exercises sent to your phone.

Gentle walking is often advised in the first days after treatment. Photo: Biel Morro (CC0).

Split home exercises by time: the first 24 hours, at your desk, days 2–7, week 2 onward, and if it gets worse. Patients reopen this page on their phone every evening.

Two or three simple stretches a day help keep progress between visits. Photo: Patrick Hendry (CC0).

5. Prices, care plans and insurance

Show the price of the new patient exam, the follow-up visit and care plans of 6 or 10 visits. Say plans are optional and unused visits are refunded, so nobody feels pushed.

Add one line about insurance for where patients live. A clinic near a border might write: US patients can use HSA/FSA; Canadian patients can claim receipts on extended health benefits.

6. Booking by open time

The booking form shows only times with enough room for the chosen service, based on each day’s hours. Let patients pick a practitioner, the problem (filled in when they tap “Book for this”), a few health questions and the red-flag check. Requests go to Google Forms, email or a message.

If you also take bookings for a spa, gym or sports court, see how to build a free booking website. Massage studios can read the massage shop website guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can a chiropractor website say it cures conditions?

Better not. Describe what you do, how many visits it usually takes and when to see a doctor. Avoid cure claims or comparisons with medication; health advertising has its own rules in many places.

Should prices be public?

At least the new patient exam and follow-up visit. New patients hesitate to call about price; clear prices make booking easier.

How do I keep patients with red flags from booking?

List the warning signs that need a doctor or the ER first and repeat them as a question in the booking form. If someone ticks one, the page advises seeing a doctor now.

Does a small clinic need a website if it has social media?

Yes. Social media is good for tips and photos; a website answers what patients ask before they come: what you treat, how many visits, the price and how to book.
