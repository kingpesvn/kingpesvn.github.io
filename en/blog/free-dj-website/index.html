Free DJ website: availability calendar and quotes

How to make a free DJ website: let clients hear your mixes, compare wedding and event packages, pick a free date and get an instant quote with travel.

Clients booking a DJ for a wedding or event usually ask three things: are you free that day, what kind of music do you play, and how much does it cost. Answering every message one by one takes hours, and the client still has to wait. This guide shows how to build a free DJ website from a ready-made template, so clients can hear your mixes, check your free dates and get a quote right on the page.

Quick summary, 5 things to know:

  1. What a DJ website needs
  2. Mixes clients can preview
  3. Packages with clear prices
  4. Free dates and upcoming shows
  5. An instant quote when clients book

1. What does a DJ website need?

A DJ who plays weddings, private parties or clubs needs a page that shows their music style, experience and how to book. The DJ website template has a dark background, bold capital letters and stage-light photos, plus an about section, years behind the decks, number of events and the venues you have played.

A shot of the event mood, taken from behind the audience, shows your style without revealing guests’ faces. Photo: Laura Wielo (CC0).

At the top, a small strip shows how many dates are still free this month and your next public show.

2. Mixes clients can preview

Clients want to listen before they book. Each mix has a cover image, genres, BPM, length and a button that opens SoundCloud, Mixcloud or YouTube. Clients tap a genre chip such as House, Pop or Retro to see only the mixes that suit their party.

Have at least one mix for weddings and one for clubs, because these two groups look for very different music.

3. Packages with clear prices

The template has four packages: wedding, private party, club and corporate event. Each shows a price, the hours included, the price per extra hour and what is included, such as a playlist meeting, a wireless microphone or music cues for each moment of the program.

Clear packages help clients compare quickly and choose what they really need. Photo: Catboy69 (CC0).

Your most popular package gets a highlight label so new clients know where to start.

4. Free dates and upcoming shows

The calendar shows the next three months. Dates you have already booked are crossed out, past dates are faded and free dates can be clicked; a click fills that date into the booking form. The list of upcoming shows hides gigs that have already happened.

The calendar is only accurate if you update it: every time you take a gig, add the date to the booked list and republish.

5. An instant quote when clients book

The booking form estimates the price from the package, hours, guest count, extras and distance. The sound system is picked by guest count, for example speakers for up to 200 guests; travel is charged after the free kilometres; the deposit at the bottom uses the percentage you set.

Pricing by guest count helps clients understand why a big party needs a bigger sound system. Photo: beeki (CC0).

Booked dates show a warning and cannot be sent. Requests arrive by WhatsApp, email or Google Forms. If you are a singer or band, read websites for musicians and see the singer and band template.

Frequently asked questions

Does a DJ need a website if I already have Facebook and TikTok?

Social media helps people find you; a website helps them book you. On your site, clients see free dates, packages and prices at once and send a complete request, so you don’t have to ask the same questions again.

Should I show prices publicly?

Show a starting price for each package and say clearly that it is an estimate. Clients filter themselves by budget, and those who message you are usually ready to book.

Is it a lot of work to keep the calendar up to date?

No. Each time you take a gig, add that date to the list of booked dates in the editor and republish. The calendar on the page crosses it out automatically.

Can mixes play right on the website?

The template links each mix to SoundCloud, Mixcloud or YouTube. This keeps the site light and avoids streaming rights issues on your own page.
